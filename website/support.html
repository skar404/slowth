Apps · iPhone & iPad only

Block Reels and Shorts inside apps.

To block Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts or short videos in Facebook, X (Twitter) and other social apps, select the app and follow the steps below. You can also select apps such as Snapchat, TikTok and games for whole-app blocking. See how blocking works without monitoring.

  1. Open the Apps tab and allow Screen Time access when prompted. This lets Slowth ask iOS to shield selected apps. It does not start a recording.
  2. Choose the apps you want to block in Apple’s Screen Time picker. Categories select the apps currently in them; only apps are saved, and website selections are ignored. Select a category again after installing more apps.
  3. Tap an app to choose its rules for short videos, Stories and soft blocking. New selections block both content types, with soft blocking off.
  4. Tap Start monitoring in the Apps tab, select Slowth in Apple’s broadcast prompt if needed, and confirm the start. Wait for monitoring to become active before returning to an app.
  5. When selected content is detected, Screen Time shields the whole app. Follow the short rest countdown, then leave that content when you return.
Recognition depends on the screen. Slowth recognizes short-video and Stories screens across social apps, including Instagram, YouTube, Facebook and X (Twitter). App layouts change, and recognition can miss content or block ordinary screens by mistake. Selecting an app alone does not start monitoring.

Understand screen access

The broadcast supplies images of your current screen, potentially including other apps and sensitive information. App selection controls which apps are blocked; it does not limit what appears in the broadcast. In the App Store app, frames are processed temporarily on-device, not saved or uploaded. Audio is ignored.

Stop monitoring

Return to Slowth, tap Stop monitoring and confirm in the system prompt. You can also use the iOS recording indicator to stop the broadcast. Check that the indicator has stopped. Stopping monitoring changes app protection as explained below; it does not turn off the Safari extension.

Safari · iPhone, iPad & Mac

Start with your browser.

iPhone and iPad

  1. Install and open Slowth.
  2. In iOS Settings, open Apps → Safari → Extensions → Slowth and enable the extension. On older iOS versions, Safari is directly in Settings.
  3. Allow access to the supported websites you want Slowth to filter. Use All Websites → Allow if you want to cover all supported sites.
  4. Open Slowth’s Safari settings and choose the content to block per site. Reload any pages already open in Safari.

Mac

  1. Open Safari → Settings → Extensions and enable Slowth.
  2. Allow website access when Safari asks.
  3. Choose your per-site options in Slowth and reload the page.

Safari does not need Screen Time permission or a screen broadcast. YouTube offers Shorts controls; Instagram and Facebook offer Reels and feed controls; X covers Explore and trends. Supported websites can also be blocked entirely. TikTok has whole-site blocking.

Use Update rules now to check for changes to the public blocking rules. Rule updates require a network connection and cannot be applied during Strict Mode.

When recording is off.

Selected protected apps are normally shielded without a running broadcast. Stopping recording does not give unrestricted access to those apps.

Soft blocking
If enabled for an app, Slowth briefly allows it after you leave Slowth, then asks iOS to shield it when it is used. This can help with background audio. Picture in Picture can still be blocked; it is not an unrestricted mode.
Temporary access
Allow content opens the whole selected group for the pause duration. It works without recording. When access ends, blocking resumes, although iOS may delay applying it.

To use content recognition, start monitoring again. If you cannot change a setting, check whether Strict Mode is active.

Two different kinds of pause.

A rest after detection

After Slowth detects content you chose to block, a short countdown gives you a moment away from the app. These automatic rests last 5–15 seconds and do not use leaves. You can close Slowth while the countdown continues. Leave the blocked content when you return, or another rest may begin.

Temporary access with a leaf

On iPhone and iPad, apps and Safari share three pauses per day. Allow content uses one leaf to temporarily allow blocked content across the selected group. Under Settings → Pause duration, choose 30 seconds, 1 minute or 3 minutes for new pauses. The timer shows the remaining access; Block again ends it early without refunding the leaf.

Unused leaves become a saved reward count after the day ends; saved leaves are not additional access. Shared pauses are experimental, and iOS may delay restoring restrictions. A screen broadcast is not required for temporary access.

Strict Mode, with clear limits.

Strict Mode locks enabled restrictions for 24 hours. The timer survives closing the app and restarting the device. You cannot end it early inside Slowth.

  • You cannot weaken enabled restrictions. You can add restrictions, but cannot enable whole-site blocking during the lock.
  • Rule refresh is locked while Strict Mode is active.
  • On iPhone and iPad, decide whether to Allow pauses in Strict mode before starting. If pauses are disallowed, you cannot re-enable them during the lock.

Strict Mode is a personal commitment tool. It does not make the device tamper-proof or prevent every change outside Slowth, such as removing the app or changing system permissions. Review your settings before enabling it.

Something not quite right?

Shorts or Reels still appear in Safari

Check that the extension is enabled, has permission for that website and has the relevant blocking option enabled. Reload the page. Outside Strict Mode, update rules and try again. Websites change their layout; if it still fails, send the site name, OS version and steps to reproduce.

An app is blocked as soon as I open it

Check the monitoring state in Slowth. Without an active broadcast, selected protected apps are normally blocked. Start monitoring for content detection, or use a temporary-access pause if available. Also check whether a detection rest is still active.

Monitoring stops, or does not start

Return to Slowth and start monitoring again through Apple’s prompt. Check the system recording indicator and Screen Time permission. iOS can interrupt screen broadcasts; Slowth cannot silently restart one for you. Report repeated interruptions with your device and OS version.

Slowth blocks the wrong screen or misses content

Recognition is imperfect. Check the selected app’s content rules and whether its content is supported. You can report what happened through the app’s feedback option or by email. Do not send sensitive screen content; a description of the app, screen and steps is a useful start.

The pause ended, but access did not change immediately

The pause timer and the system shield are separate: iOS may delay restoring restrictions. Reopen Slowth to check its current state. Include the pause duration and whether monitoring was running when reporting the issue.

A setting is locked or an app is missing

Check the Strict Mode timer and review your selection in the system picker. Only apps are saved from the system picker; websites are ignored. If you selected a category before installing an app, select the category again.

Still need a hand?

Tell me what happened.

Email denis@malina.page or open a GitHub issue. Include the Slowth version, device and OS version, steps to reproduce, and what you expected. Mention whether it happened in Safari or inside an app, and whether monitoring, a pause or Strict Mode was active.
